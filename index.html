        Secondary hazards

          Observed vs modelled — early signal

          The best-documented damage is in Penonomé (Coclé), where the ShakeMap estimates only MMI ~VI. That points either to stronger local shaking than modelled (site amplification, rupture directivity toward the north-east) or to the particular vulnerability of older heritage and commercial structures. Conversely, there are very few reports yet from southern Azuero (Tonosí, Macaracas, Las Minas, Los Pozos) where modelled shaking is strongest (MMI VIII–IX) — likely an information gap (power and telecom outages, rural areas) rather than an absence of damage.

          About this page

          This page is a preliminary, unofficial compilation to support early situational awareness after the 9 October 2026 earthquake in Panama. It brings together publicly available information from the internet: the USGS earthquake products, news reports, official statements as relayed by the media, and social media posts. No formal damage, loss or risk assessment has been carried out. Figures labelled "official" are those reported by Panamanian authorities as quoted in public sources; all other numbers are either media reports or model estimates and will change.

          This is not a product of the Government of Panama, the World Bank or any other institution, and it does not represent their views.

          Compiled by Brenden Jongman, with AI-assisted search and data processing. Corrections and additional sources are welcome.

          How it updates

          • Earthquakes and aftershocks are loaded live from the USGS API in your browser every 2 minutes.
          • News stream, USGS PAGER/ShakeMap status, GDACS are refreshed automatically every 15 minutes.
          • Curated impact reports, official figures and the overview text are reviewed and updated regularly by searching news, official channels and social media; every item links to its source.

          Data sources

            Known limitations

            • Media reports in the first hours are often incomplete, duplicated or wrong. Items are tagged by confidence; unconfirmed claims (e.g. trapped persons) are marked as such.
            • Locations are geocoded to town level and are approximate.
            • Areas with power and telecom outages are under-reported.
            • Exposure uses an automatic ShakeMap with a point-source rupture; it will change when USGS publishes a finite-fault model.
            • Building footprints from machine learning miss buildings under tree cover and in remote areas; counts are lower-to-mid estimates.
            • Images are hot-linked from their original publishers and remain their property.
